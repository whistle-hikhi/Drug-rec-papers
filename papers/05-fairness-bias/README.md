# Fairness & Bias in Drug/Medication Recommendation

Added 2026-08-19 to close the gap flagged in [`NOTES.md`](../../NOTES.md): none of the original four areas surfaced anything on demographic bias in recommended medications. This category is cross-cutting by nature — it applies to EHR-based models, KG/GNN methods, and LLMs alike — so it's organized by *what kind of harm/evidence* rather than by which of the other four areas it touches.

**Update (2026-09-08)**: the gap noted below — "no paper evaluates fairness on the exact medication-recommendation task" — has partially closed. Two papers, **RAREMed** and **DMRNet**, now do exactly this, framing rare-vs-common medication accuracy as a fairness problem within the GAMENet/SafeDrug-style task itself (see new section A0). It's a narrower fix than *demographic* fairness (both are about drug-frequency fairness, not race/gender/age subgroup fairness), so demographic-subgroup fairness on the actual recommendation task still looks open — but "fairness on the task" as a category is no longer empty. What exists across the rest of this page: (a) the landmark case showing algorithmic bias in a *related* clinical-risk task, (b) fairness studies on the *closest adjacent task* (opioid/substance-use prediction and medication adherence, both prescribing-relevant), (c) general clinical-prediction fairness methodology, (d) LLM/clinical-NLP bias benchmarks, and (e) fairness-aware recommender-systems methodology transferable to the drug-rec setting.

## A0. Fairness *on* the medication-recommendation task itself — the closest matches found

| Year | Paper | Venue | Key idea | Link |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2024 | Leave No Patient Behind: Enhancing Medication Recommendation for Rare Disease Patients (RAREMed) | arXiv | Explicitly frames the accuracy gap between common- and rare-disease patients as a **fairness issue** in medication recommendation, not just a long-tail performance problem. Transformer encoder + pretrain-finetune (Sequence Matching Prediction, Self Reconstruction) evaluated on real-world medication-recommendation datasets, showing improved accuracy for rare-disease patients without sacrificing common-disease accuracy | [arXiv:2403.17745](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.17745) |
| 2026 | DMRNet: Debiased Medication Recommendation Through Fusing Frequent Pattern and Temporal Medical Records | PubMed | Directly targets the same bias mechanism as RAREMed — models skew toward common medications, degrading rare-drug predictions — via (1) frequent-pattern mining across diverse drug combinations to rebalance training signal, and (2) a drug-recalibration mechanism that selectively fuses temporal prescription records to boost low-frequency drug recall | [PubMed:42214931](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42214931/) |

**Read these two together** — same problem (frequency-bias fairness within the recommendation task), two years apart, different mechanisms (pretrain-finetune representation learning vs. frequent-pattern-mining + recalibration). Worth comparing whether DMRNet cites/benchmarks against RAREMed directly. Neither addresses *demographic* fairness (race/gender/age) — that intersection with the actual recommendation task remains unaddressed as far as this collection has found; see the note at the bottom.

## Notes for later reading

- **The gap has partially closed (2026-09-08 update).** RAREMed and DMRNet (section A0) now evaluate fairness directly on the medication-recommendation task itself — but both are about *drug-frequency* fairness (rare vs. common medications), not *demographic* fairness. A subgroup-broken-down (race/gender/age) fairness audit of the actual GAMENet/SafeDrug/COGNet-style combinatorial task still does not appear to exist in the literature searched. If your PhD contribution needs a demographic-fairness angle specifically, that narrower intersection — DDI-safety-constrained recommendation *and* demographic subgroup fairness, evaluated jointly — still looks like genuine white space. Worth a further search (ACM FAccT, health-informatics venues directly) before concluding it's fully open.
- **Read Obermeyer et al. (2019) first regardless of angle** — it's the reference point nearly every other paper here either cites or structurally mirrors (proxy-variable bias via a cost/utilization stand-in for need). Understanding its mechanism makes the rest of section A-C easier to place.
- **RAREMed and DMRNet (A0) are now the most directly relevant reads** if fairness is a live angle for your work — they're the only two papers here evaluated on the actual recommendation task rather than an adjacent one. Read them right after the GAMENet→MoleRec spine in `01-ehr-based`, since both build on that same architectural lineage.
- **Opioid-prescribing bias and medication-adherence fairness (section B) remain the best proxies for *demographic* fairness in drug-related prediction** — subgroup fairness has actually been measured against ML predictions there, even though both are risk/eligibility/adherence prediction tasks rather than the combinatorial recommendation task itself. The new prior-authorization fairness paper (Anterior) is a reminder that even a demographically fair recommendation can produce unfair real-world outcomes if the downstream approval step isn't audited too.
- **Section C's methodology is directly reusable**: the EHR-continuity-as-bias-source finding (arXiv:2309.01935) and the matched-counterpart method both transfer cleanly to auditing `01-ehr-based` models on MIMIC, where data missingness/continuity is known to correlate with demographics.
- **Cross-reference with `04-llm-generative`**: the RAG-bias paper (arXiv:2503.15454) is a direct fairness lens on the RAG architectures already collected there (EHR-RAG, PACE-RAG, etc.) — read together. CFaiRLLM (section E) is the matching methodology paper if that lens becomes a full audit.
