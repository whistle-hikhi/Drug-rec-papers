# Evaluation Datasets Beyond MIMIC

Added 2026-08-19 to close the gap flagged in [`NOTES.md`](../../NOTES.md): every model in `01-ehr-based` was benchmarked on MIMIC-III/IV, which is a single US academic-hospital ICU population — a known field-wide limitation (single institution, single country, ICU-only, so results may not transfer to general wards, primary care, or non-US health systems). This category tracks the non-MIMIC datasets and papers that actually use them, organized by what kind of "beyond MIMIC" they offer: multi-center public data, non-US/non-English datasets, and private single-institution data used for external validation.

**Bottom line so far**: the field has *some* multi-center generalization testing (mainly via eICU) and a growing non-English thread (Chinese hospital data), but true external validation against MIMIC-trained models — training on MIMIC, testing elsewhere, reporting the performance drop — is still rare. Most "multi-dataset" papers train and test separately per dataset rather than testing cross-dataset transfer directly.

## A. Multi-center public ICU data (the most common MIMIC alternative)

| Year | Paper / resource | Venue | Key idea | Link |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2018 | The eICU Collaborative Research Database, a Freely Available Multi-Center Database for Critical Care Research | Scientific Data | The dataset itself: ICU records from 208 US hospitals, released 2018. The standard non-MIMIC public alternative — but far sparser per-hospital (most hospitals have under 1,500 records vs. MIMIC-III's 5,000+ from one hospital), and lacks procedure codes, which several medication-rec papers have to work around | [PhysioNet](https://physionet.org/content/eicu-crd/2.0/) |
| 2023 | DKINet: Medication Recommendation via Domain Knowledge Informed Deep Learning | arXiv | Explicitly evaluates on **MIMIC-III, MIMIC-IV, and eICU** in one paper — the clearest existing example of testing a medication-rec model across more than one data source | [arXiv:2305.19604](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.19604) |
| 2024/2025 | A Contrastive Pretrain Model with Prompt Tuning for Multi-Center Medication Recommendation (TEMPT) | ACM TOIS | Framed *specifically* around the multi-center problem: motivates that most work assumes one data-rich hospital, while real hospitals vary widely in data volume/distribution; pretrains on shared structure then prompt-tunes per hospital using eICU's genuine multi-hospital structure. Already in [`04-llm-generative`](../04-llm-generative/README.md#fine-tuning-alignment--distillation) — cross-referenced here because the dataset framing is the more relevant angle for this gap. | [arXiv:2412.20040](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.20040) |

## B. Non-US / non-English hospital & claims datasets

| Year | Paper / resource | Venue | Key idea | Link |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2015 | Data Resource Profile: Clinical Practice Research Datalink (CPRD) | International Journal of Epidemiology | UK primary-care longitudinal EHR covering ~50M patients, up to 20 years follow-up — the standard non-US, non-ICU (primary care, not hospital) alternative. No medication-recommendation paper found yet that actually benchmarks on CPRD directly; flagged here as an available resource rather than a used one | [PMC4521131](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4521131/) |
| 2018 | Data Resource Profile: The National Health Insurance Research Database (NHIRD) of Taiwan | — | Population-scale (near 100% of Taiwan's population) administrative claims data — diagnoses, prescriptions, procedures, hospital visits. A claims-based (not chart-based) alternative, structurally different from MIMIC/eICU/CPRD; widely used in pharmacoepidemiology but not found used yet for combinatorial medication-*recommendation* modeling specifically | [PMC6367203](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6367203/) |
| 2021 | Optimal Treatment Recommendations for Diabetes Patients Using the Markov Decision Process along with the South Korean Electronic Health Records | — | Non-US, non-English EHR (Korean National Health Insurance Sharing Service data); RL/MDP framing rather than the GAMENet-style deep-learning lineage, and disease-specific (diabetes) rather than general — still a useful non-MIMIC data point on treatment recommendation | [PMC7994640](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7994640/) |
| 2025 | Overview of CHIP 2025 Shared Task 2: Discharge Medication Recommendation for Metabolic Diseases Based on Chinese Electronic Health Records | arXiv | Introduces **CDrugRed**, a purpose-built Chinese medication-recommendation dataset (5,894 de-identified hospitalization records, 3,190 patients) plus a shared-task leaderboard (top team: Jaccard 0.51, F1 0.63 with an LLM-ensemble system) — the closest thing found to a genuinely new, non-MIMIC, medication-recommendation-specific benchmark rather than a repurposed general clinical dataset | [arXiv:2511.06230](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.06230) |
| 2025 | MediRec: Enhancing Chinese Medication Recommendation with Explainable Clinical Reasoning | arXiv | Uses the CDrugRed-related Chinese dataset above; benchmarks LLM families (GLM, Llama, Qwen) with reasoning-chain distillation + RL. ## C. Private single-institution datasets used for external validation

| Year | Paper | Venue | Key idea | Link |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2021 | PREMIER: Personalizing Medication Recommendation with a Graph-Based Approach | ACM TOIS | Evaluates on **MIMIC-III *and* a private hospital dataset** that lacks procedure codes entirely — GAMENet and a comparison model had to be adapted to run without procedure codes to make the comparison possible. The private dataset's baseline DDI rate (0.196) is reported directly, letting you compare "real-world" interaction prevalence against MIMIC's. One of the few papers that shows what breaks (missing procedure codes) when you leave MIMIC's schema | [ACM TOIS](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3488668) |

## Notes for later reading

- **eICU is the practical next step if you want multi-institution results without new data access** — it's public, and DKINet already demonstrates the MIMIC-III/MIMIC-IV/eICU three-way comparison pattern worth replicating or extending.
- **The sparsity problem is the real story with eICU**, not just "another dataset exists" — most eICU hospitals have under 1,500 records vs. MIMIC-III's 5,000+, so eICU functions as a *stress test* for data-sparse settings (this is exactly what TEMPT's prompt-tuning approach targets) rather than a like-for-like swap-in benchmark.
- **CPRD and NHIRD are available but apparently unused** for medication-*recommendation* modeling specifically (as opposed to pharmacoepidemiology generally) — this looks like an actual opportunity rather than just a collection gap: applying the GAMENet/SafeDrug lineage to claims data (NHIRD) or non-ICU primary care data (CPRD) would be a genuinely new data regime for this literature, with structurally different input (claims lack clinical narrative/labs; primary care has multi-year longitudinal history that ICU stays don't).
- **PREMIER's private-dataset adaptation (dropping procedure codes) is a concrete methodological warning**: several EHR-based models assume MIMIC's exact schema (diagnosis + procedure + medication codes); porting to another EHR system often requires deciding how to handle fields that don't exist there. Worth reading before assuming any classical model from `01-ehr-based` "just works" on new data.
- **Still an open gap**: no paper found trains on MIMIC and evaluates zero-shot/fine-tuned on a *different* dataset to directly report the performance drop (true external/transfer validation, as opposed to training-and-testing separately per dataset). If cross-dataset generalization is part of your framing, that experiment itself looks novel.
